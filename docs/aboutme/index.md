#About Me

Ryan Thomas

I'm a third-year Mechanical Engineering student at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte, currently working as an Engineering Intern at AK Performance and previously as an Assistant Broadcast Engineer. My interest in engineering started long before I knew what it was. Growing up, I was always building things and taking them apart, whether through woodworking projects, Legos, repairs, or home improvements. I was more interested in understanding why things were designed the way they were than in simply finishing them. That instinct, paired with my lifelong interest in motorsports, pointed me toward mechanical engineering, with a focus on motorsports. That interest has only sharpened through my coursework, including statics and other engineering-related classes. These courses stood out to me because they gave me a rigorous way to answer questions I used to think over only briefly, such as how much load passes through a given point in a structure or beam, where a component is most likely to fail, or why a particular geometry was chosen over another alternative. I have come to enjoy that level of specificity, knowing the general operation of something versus knowing the precise ways and reasons it works. In the future, I am working toward becoming a race engineer or performance data engineer in sports car racing. Those goals have shaped how I approach my education. I now spend much of my free time studying race car dynamics alongside my classwork, building the technical foundation I will need to translate engineering fundamentals into real performance gains on track wherever I end up.

"What does it mean to defend an engineering decision, and do you currently know how to do it?"

For me, defending an engineering decision comes down to several factors. Once you lay out your design and get a general idea of what you want to do, the first step is to present multiple options for comparison. That helps define the strengths and weaknesses of each option and lets you go into more depth on them to show why one option is better than the others through math, materials, etc. So, in short, defending an engineering idea means laying out one idea and defending it against multiple other plausible, similar ideas. As for whether I currently know how to do it, I would say no, but I think I have some understanding. In my current work, I work under a crew chief, and I need to bring up setup changes. I've also had a little experience backing up those ideas and showing the data we are provided in track logs. So, to an extent, I know how to do it, but I wouldn't say I'm an expert. For the sake of this class, I would say no.
